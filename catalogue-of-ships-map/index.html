The Catalogue of Ships, Mapped

Iliad Book 2’s Catalogue of Ships (lines 494–759) names every Achaean contingent at Troy — dozens of leaders, cities, and ship counts, read in a block that most editions leave as a wall of proper names. The Homer Reader turns it into an explorable map: every contingent is a pin.

What the map shows

  • Click a pin for a card listing that contingent’s leader(s), ship count, and home places — with a direct jump to its lines in the text.
  • Every place identification is graded by a certainty tier — certain, traditional, speculative, or mythical — so the map is honest about how much the ancient geography is actually known versus inferred by later tradition.
  • The Catalogue is one of four maps in the reader, alongside the Troad, Odysseus’s wanderings, and the real Greece of the poems; place names in the reading text link to the same pins.

Questions

What is the Catalogue of Ships?

The long roster near the start of Iliad Book 2 (lines 494–759) that lists every Achaean contingent that sailed to Troy: its leader, its home cities, and how many ships it sent.

How reliable are the place identifications on the map?

Each place carries a certainty tier — certain, traditional, speculative, or mythical — and any identification made by later tradition rather than the poem itself names that tradition. The map never states an identification with more confidence than the evidence supports.

Can I jump from the map to the text?

Yes — every contingent’s card links straight to its lines in Iliad Book 2, and the explorer covers three other maps besides the Catalogue: the Troad, Odysseus’s wanderings, and the real Greece of the poems.
