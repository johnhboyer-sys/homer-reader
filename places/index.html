  2. Abydos

    Ἄβυδος certain

    City on the Asian shore of the Hellespont, the narrowest crossing point; home of Trojan allies under Asius.

    Pleiades Il. 2.836

  3. Adrasteia

    Ἀδρήστεια traditional

    Troad-Propontis town giving its name to the plain of Adrasteia and, later, to the epithet of Nemesis/Adrasteia.

    Tradition: Plain and town near modern Karacabey/Erdek area, southern Propontis coast (Strabo 13.1.13)

    Il. 2.828

  4. Aeaea

    Αἰαίη traditional

    Island of the enchantress Circe, who transforms Odysseus's men into swine. Roman-era tradition (Theophrastus HP 5.8.3; later Latin poets) locates it at Monte Circeo on the Italian coast.

    Tradition: Monte Circeo (ancient Circeii), Italy (Theophrastus HP 5.8.3; Roman literary tradition)

    Pleiades Od. 10.135

  5. Aegialus (Achaea)

    Αἰγιαλός certain

    "The coastland": Homer's name for the whole Corinthian Gulf shore later called Achaea.

    Il. 2.575

  6. Aegialus (Paphlagonia)

    Αἰγιαλός speculative

    "The coastland" of Paphlagonia; a generic descriptive name shared with the Achaean-side Aegialus (Il. 2.575).

    Tradition: Paphlagonian coast, 'the shoreland,' homonymous with the Achaean Aegialus in this Catalogue; Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.855

  7. Aegilips

    Αἰγίλιψ speculative

    "Rugged" place in Odysseus's kingdom that Strabo could not identify with confidence.

    Tradition: Cephallenian/Ithacan territory, exact site unknown (Strabo 10.2.10); Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.633

  8. Aegina

    Αἴγινα certain

    Saronic Gulf island-city; modern Aegina preserves the name over the ancient site.

    Il. 2.562

  9. Aegium

    Αἴγιον certain

    Chief city of Achaea, meeting-place of the Achaean League; modern Aigio occupies the ancient site.

    Il. 2.574

  10. Aeolia

    Αἰολίη traditional

    Floating island home of Aeolus, keeper of the winds, who gives Odysseus the bag of storm-winds. Ancient tradition (Thucydides 3.88) identifies it with the Aeolian (Lipari) Islands.

    Tradition: Aeolian/Lipari Islands off Sicily (Thucydides 3.88)

    Od. 10.1

  11. Aesepus (river)

    Αἴσηπος certain

    River of the northern Troad flowing from Ida past Zeleia; the modern Gönen Çayı.

    Il. 2.825

  12. Aethices

    Αἴθικες speculative

    Mountain people on Thessaly's western fringe, near the Perrhaebians and Pindus; territory only vaguely fixed.

    Tradition: A people/district on the western Thessalian-Epirote border, near Mt Pindus, exact bounds unknown (Strabo 9.5.11); Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.744

  13. Aetolia

    Αἰτωλοί certain

    Region of west-central Greece around Calydon; named as the homeland of the man who tricks Eumaeus's false Cretan into a lie about Odysseus.

    Od. 14.379Il. 2.638

  14. Aipy

    Αἰπύ speculative

    "Steep" town in Triphylian territory; Strabo notes only the descriptive name, not a firm site.

    Tradition: Triphylia, 'the steep town,' exact site unknown (Strabo 8.3.24); Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.592

  15. Alesium

    Ἀλήσιον speculative

    Elean landmark Strabo tentatively connects with a hill called Alesiaeum; unconfirmed.

    Tradition: Elis, possibly a hill near Amphidolis, exact site unknown (Strabo 8.3.10); Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.617

  16. Alope

    Ἀλόπη speculative

    Small Achaea Phthiotis town grouped with Alus and Trachis; unlocated precisely.

    Tradition: Achaea Phthiotis, near Alus, exact site unknown (Strabo 9.5.8); Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.682

  17. Alus

    Ἄλος traditional

    Achaea Phthiotis town near the Malian Gulf; site associated with modern Almyros/Halos.

    Tradition: Thessalian coastal town near modern Almyros, on the Malian Gulf road (Strabo 9.5.8)

    Il. 2.682

  18. Alybe

    Ἀλύβη speculative

    Named only for its silver; ancient scholarship already treated its location as an open, much-debated problem.

    Tradition: "Birthplace of silver": among the most famously unlocated Homeric place-names, disputed since antiquity (Strabo 12.3.20-27)

    Il. 2.857

  19. Amphigeneia

    Ἀμφιγένεια speculative

    Triphylian town with a sanctuary of Leto per Strabo; precise site unconfirmed.

    Tradition: Triphylia, near the Hypsoeis river, exact site unknown (Strabo 8.3.25); Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.593

  20. Amyclae

    Ἀμύκλαι certain

    Excavated sanctuary of Apollo south of Sparta; the pre-Dorian settlement absorbed into Lacedaemon.

    Il. 2.584

  21. Amydon

    Ἀμυδών speculative

    Paeonian city on the Axius, already legendary/vanished by the time of later Greek geography.

    Tradition: Paeonia, on the lower Axius, said destroyed before historical times (Strabo 12.3.20, fr.); Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.849

  22. Anemoreia

    Ἀνεμώρεια speculative

    "Windy" Phocian settlement Strabo says was later renamed Anemolea; precise site unconfirmed.

    Tradition: Windy pass site near Delphi, later called Anemolea per Strabo 9.3.15; Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.521

  23. Anthedon

    Ἀνθηδών traditional

    "Farthest-lying" Boeotian coastal town facing Euboea; site located near modern Loukisia.

    Tradition: Coastal site near modern Loukisia, East Boeotia (Strabo 9.2.13; Paus. 9.22.5)

    Il. 2.508

  24. Antron

    Ἄντρων traditional

    Coastal Achaea Phthiotis town facing Euboea across the strait; approximate site near modern Glypha.

    Tradition: Achaea Phthiotis coast opposite Euboea, near modern Glypha (Strabo 9.5.14)

    Il. 2.697

  25. Apaesus

    Ἄπαισος speculative

    Small Propontis-coast town Strabo says was later called Paesus; precise site unconfirmed.

    Tradition: Propontis coast near Adrasteia, later renamed Paesus/Apaesus, exact site unknown (Strabo 13.1.19); Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.828

  26. Araethyrea

    Ἀραιθυρέη speculative

    "Lovely" district Strabo identifies with the later city of Phlius; precise Homeric-era site unconfirmed.

    Tradition: Sicyonian territory, later called Phlius per Strabo 8.6.24; Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.571

  27. Arene

    Ἀρήνη speculative

    Town Strabo could not confidently place, near the Minyeius in Triphylia; disputed since antiquity.

    Tradition: Messenia/Triphylia, near the Minyeius river, exact site disputed (Strabo 8.3.19); Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.591

  28. Argissa

    Ἄργισσα traditional

    Lapith town on the Peneius near later Larissa; Strabo notes some identified it with Argura.

    Tradition: On the Peneius, near modern Larissa, Thessaly, sometimes identified with Argura (Strabo 9.5.19)

    Il. 2.738

  29. Argos

    Ἄργος certain

    City of the Argolid; Homer also uses 'Argos' loosely for the wider Peloponnese/Achaea, a usage noted since antiquity.

    Pleiades Od. 4.174Il. 2.559

  30. Arisbe

    Ἀρίσβη traditional

    Asius's home city on the Propontis coast, colonized in tradition from Mytilene.

    Il. 2.836

  31. Arne

    Ἄρνη speculative

    Boeotian city Strabo says may have been swallowed by Lake Copais; identification disputed since antiquity.

    Tradition: Possibly submerged under Lake Copais or identified with Acraephnium (Strabo 9.2.34-35); Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.507

  32. Ascania (Phrygia)

    Ἀσκανίη traditional

    District named for a lake in northwest Phrygia/Bithynia, generally identified with modern Lake İznik.

    Il. 2.863

  33. Asine

    Il. 2.560

  34. Aspledon

    Ἀσπληδών traditional

    Il. 2.511

  35. Asterium

  42. Batieia

    Βατίεια speculative

    A mound before Troy that men call Batieia but the gods call the barrow of swift Myrina (Iliad 2.811-814); a real narrated landmark within the story, not merely mythical, but its physical site is not securely identified.

    Tradition: A tumulus on the Trojan plain, one of several (with the tombs of Ilus and Aesyetes) discussed in Troad topography since Strabo (13.1.36-37); no ancient or modern survey (e.g. Leaf, Troy: A Study in Homeric Geography, 1912) securely matches a surviving mound to Batieia.

    Il. 2.813–814

  43. Bessa

    Βῆσσα speculative

    "Wooded glen" Locrian town, likewise vanished by antiquity per Strabo; unlocated.

    Tradition: East Locris, exact site unknown; also lost by Strabo's time (Strabo 9.4.5); Simpson–Lazenby, The Catalogue of the Ships in Homer's Iliad (1970)

    Il. 2.532

  44. Boagrius (river)
