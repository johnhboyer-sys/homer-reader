Timeline

A day-by-day chronology of the Iliad and Odyssey, derived from each book’s scene apparatus. Summarized spans — stretches the poem itself compresses into a handful of lines — draw narrower and hatched, with their day-count labeled; dramatized days draw full width.

Day-counts vary in the scholarship — this timeline follows the traditional reading of 51 Iliad days (also cited as 50, 52, or 54 by inclusive/exclusive counting of the summarized spans) and a Day 1–41 Odyssey calendar, one day ahead of Delebecque’s reported Day-33 telling; treat totals as citable convention, not settled consensus.

  1. 8 days compressed Nine days of plague

    ἐννῆμαρ … τῇ δεκάτῃ

    Apollo’s arrows range through the camp for nine days; on the tenth, Achilles calls the host to assembly.

    Il. 1.53–54 — jump to the passage

  2. 10 days compressed Twelve-day divine absence

    ἀλλ᾽ ὅτε δή ῥ᾽ ἐκ τοῖο δυωδεκάτη γένετ᾽ ἠώς

    The gods return from feasting among the Aethiopians on the twelfth dawn, and Thetis makes her plea to Zeus.

    Il. 1.493–494 — jump to the passage

  3. 11 days compressed Twelve-day maltreatment of Hector

    ἀλλ᾽ ὅτε δή ῥ᾽ ἐκ τοῖο δυωδεκάτη γένετ᾽ ἠώς

    The same twelfth-dawn formula recurs: Achilles drags Hector’s body around Patroclus’s tomb each night for twelve days, until Apollo protests. A further nine-day quarrel among the gods runs inside this span.

    Il. 24.31 (= 1.493); cf. 24.107 — jump to the passage

  4. 10 days compressed The truce: nine days, then the burial

    τῇ δὲ δυωδεκάτῃ πολεμίξομεν εἴ περ ἀνάγκη

    Priam’s terms: nine days of mourning, burial and feast on the tenth, a mound on the eleventh, battle again on the twelfth if it must be. The poem narrates the nine days of wood-gathering and the cremation on the tenth, but ends before reaching its own stated twelfth-day deadline — the compression is the poet’s own.

    Il. 24.667 (deadline, quoted); cf. 24.664–667, 781, 784–787 — jump to the passage

Days 1–6 (the Telemachy, Books 1–4) and Book 5’s second divine council may narrate overlapping, not strictly sequential, time — ancient epic tends to tell simultaneous events one after another rather than in parallel (Zieliński’s Law).

Day 34 — the telling

“Morning of the 34th day of the action of the poem”… “the dawn of the 35th day.” — Monro, on Od. 13.18 and 13.93 (Sources)

“The date is given with a precision most unusual in epic poetry.” — Gilbert Murray, 1924 (Sources)

The voyage — Troy to Ithaca (approximate)

Where the day strips above dramatize the poem’s narrative present, this strip approximates the decade the Odyssey compresses into that present: the ten years’ wandering from the fall of Troy to the beach at Ithaca. Stretches the poem numbers — nine days here, a month there, Circe’s year, Calypso’s seven — draw wide, sized to their stated length; stretches it never numbers draw as narrow hatched gaps. Each station is labeled and deep-linked to its first line in the reader.

  1. unstated Ismarus (the Cicones)

    The wind bears the fleet from Troy straight to Ismarus; the Cicones' city is sacked at once. No duration is stated for the passage or the sack.

    jump to the passage (Od. 9.39)

  2. unstated Cape Malea

    Wave, current, and North Wind drive the fleet off its course rounding Malea -- the last point on the direct road home. No duration stated.

    jump to the passage (Od. 9.79)

  3. unstated Cythera

    Swept past Cythera before the nine-day storm proper begins. No duration stated for this leg itself.

    jump to the passage (Od. 9.81)

  4. 9 days Land of the Lotus-eaters

    ἐννῆμαρ — 9 days, Od. 9.82

    Nine days driven by deadly winds over the fish-filled sea; on the tenth (δεκάτῃ, 9.83) they land among the Lotus-eaters.

    jump to the passage (Od. 9.82)

  5. unstated Land of the Cyclopes

    Polyphemus's cave. No duration is stated for the stay, though the blinding and its aftermath (escape under the rams, the taunting from the ships) clearly cover more than a single day.

    jump to the passage (Od. 9.105)

  6. 1 month Aeolia -- Aeolus's floating island (first landfall)

    μῆνα — 1 month, Od. 10.14

    Aeolus loves and questions Odysseus for a full month (μῆνα δὲ πάντα φίλει με, 10.14) before granting passage and the bag of bound winds.

    jump to the passage (Od. 10.1)

  7. 9 days In sight of Ithaca -- the bag opened

    ἐννῆμαρ — 9 days, Od. 10.28

    Nine days under sail, Odysseus himself steering; on the tenth their own land comes in sight (10.28-29). His crew, believing the bag holds treasure, opens it while he sleeps (10.47); the freed winds drive the fleet back to Aeolia. Not a landfall -- no placeId -- but chronologically load-bearing, so it is kept as its own station rather than folded silently into Aeolia.

    jump to the passage (Od. 10.28)

  8. unstated Aeolia (second landfall, turned away)

    Aeolus refuses further aid, judging Odysseus hateful to the gods, and sends him off at once. No duration stated for this second, brief landfall.

    jump to the passage (Od. 10.56)

  9. 6 days Laestrygonia (Telepylus)

    ἑξῆμαρ — 6 days, Od. 10.80

    Six days' sail (ἑξῆμαρ, 10.80), landfall on the seventh (ἑβδομάτῃ, 10.81). The Laestrygonians destroy eleven of the twelve ships; only Odysseus's own escapes.

    jump to the passage (Od. 10.80)

  10. 1 year Aeaea -- Circe's island

    ἐνιαυτός — 1 year, Od. 10.467-470

    A full year (ἐνιαυτός, 10.467, resumed at 10.469 ὅτε δή ῥʼ ἐνιαυτὸς ἔην) of feasting before the crew presses Odysseus to remember his own country.

    jump to the passage (Od. 10.135)

  11. unstated Land of the Cimmerians / the Underworld

    The nekyia. No duration is stated for the crossing of Ocean or the time spent at the trench; this station carries no real-world coordinates (mythical tier, per apparatus/places.json).

    jump to the passage (Od. 11.13)

  12. unstated Aeaea (return from the Underworld)

    Circe gives sailing directions for the Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis, and Thrinacia. No duration stated for this second stay.

    jump to the passage (Od. 12.1)

  13. unstated Island of the Sirens

    Passed under sail, Odysseus bound to the mast, the crew's ears sealed with wax. No duration stated.

    jump to the passage (Od. 12.39)

  14. unstated Scylla's rock

    Six of Odysseus's men are seized in passing. No duration stated -- the strait-transit with Charybdis is effectively continuous.

    jump to the passage (Od. 12.73)

  15. unstated Charybdis

    The far side of the same strait as Scylla. No duration stated.

    jump to the passage (Od. 12.101)

  16. 1 month Thrinacia -- the Cattle of the Sun

    μῆνα — 1 month, Od. 12.325-326

    A month (μῆνα, 12.325) of unrelenting South Wind pins the ship ashore. Starving, the crew kills the Sun's cattle while Odysseus sleeps; Zeus wrecks the ship on departure.

    jump to the passage (Od. 12.127)

  17. 9 days Ogygia -- Calypso's island

    ἐννῆμαρ — 9 days, Od. 12.447

    Sole survivor of the wreck at Thrinacia, Odysseus drifts nine days (ἐννῆμαρ, 12.447) before washing up on Ogygia. Calypso keeps him seven years (ἑπτάετες, 7.259, Odysseus's own retelling to Arete and Alcinous) before Hermes intervenes. Ogygia carries no coordinates in apparatus/places.json (mythical tier).

    jump to the passage (Od. 12.447)

  18. 7 years kept by Calypso

    ἑπτάετες — 7 years, Od. 7.259

    Sole survivor of the wreck at Thrinacia, Odysseus drifts nine days (ἐννῆμαρ, 12.447) before washing up on Ogygia. Calypso keeps him seven years (ἑπτάετες, 7.259, Odysseus's own retelling to Arete and Alcinous) before Hermes intervenes. Ogygia carries no coordinates in apparatus/places.json (mythical tier).

    jump to the passage (Od. 12.447)

  19. 4 days Building the raft

    τέτρατον ἦμαρ — 4 days, Od. 5.262

    Calypso supplies axe, adze, and timber; the raft is finished on the fourth day (τέτρατον ἦμαρ ἔην, 5.262), and Calypso sends Odysseus off on the fifth (τῷ δʼ ἄρα πέμπτῳ, 5.263). Still on Ogygia -- no placeId of its own.

    jump to the passage (Od. 5.228)

  20. 17 days Seventeen days under sail

    ἑπτὰ καὶ δέκα — 17 days, Od. 5.278

    Odysseus sails alone, steering by the stars as Calypso instructed. Seventeen days out (ἑπτὰ δὲ καὶ δέκα μὲν πλέεν ἤματα, 5.278); on the eighteenth (ὀκτωκαιδεκάτῃ, 5.279) the shadowy mountains of Scheria come in sight.

    jump to the passage (Od. 5.270)

  21. 2 days Poseidon's storm -- two nights adrift

    δύω νύκτας δύο τʼ ἤματα — 2 days, Od. 5.388-389

    Poseidon's wave shatters the raft (5.313-314, no number given there). Odysseus is tossed for two nights and two days (δύω νύκτας δύο τʼ ἤματα, 5.388) before the third dawn brings calm and he sights land (5.390-392). The brief's alternative citation, 5.313, is the wave-strike itself, not a duration statement -- the number is given at 5.388-389, verified here.

    jump to the passage (Od. 5.313)

  22. unstated Scheria (Phaeacia)

    Odysseus swims ashore and is found by Nausicaa; several days of hospitality, feasting, and games follow (Books 6-13) before the Phaeacians carry him home. No single number of days is stated for this whole stay.

    jump to the passage (Od. 5.441)

  23. 1 night Ithaca

    No counting-word in the Greek here — this span is read off the poem’s narrative framing, not a stated numeral.

    The Phaeacian ship sails through the night: the crew falls into a magic, deathlike sleep at the oars (13.78-80), and landfall comes as the brightest star -- herald of dawn -- rises (13.93-95). No counting-word for 'one night' appears in the Greek, unlike ἐννῆμαρ or μῆνα elsewhere on this table; the single-night span is the poem's narrative frame (evening departure to dawn arrival), not a stated numeral, and is marked approximate here.

    jump to the passage (Od. 13.78)

An honesty note. These stated spans — the nine-day storms, Aeolus’s month, Thrinacia’s month, the raft’s four days building and seventeen sailing and two nights adrift, one night’s crossing to Ithaca, Circe’s year, and Calypso’s seven — already sum to about 8.3 years, not the full ten the poem implies for the wandering. The poem itself never itemizes a ten-year total for the return: it simply asserts, four times, that Odysseus comes home in the twentieth year overall — counting, by tradition, the ten years of the war at Troy plus this decade of wandering (ἄγνωστον πάντεσσιν ἐεικοστῷ ἐνιαυτῷ — Od. 2.174-176; ἤλυθον εἰκοστῷ ἔτεϊ ἐς πατρίδα γαῖαν — Od. 16.205-206; ἤδη γάρ οἱ ἐεικοστὸν ἔτος ἐστὶν ἐξ οὗ κεῖθεν ἔβη — Od. 19.222-223; αὐτίκʼ ἰδόντʼ Ὀδυσῆα ἐεικοστῷ ἐνιαυτῷ — Od. 17.327) — while leaving the remaining months of this table entirely unstated, drawn here as the hatched gaps above. The strip’s year-scale layout is a display convention built to make that arithmetic visible, not a claim to have solved it.
