## Findings

### T1 — high — `kum-tepe` — `apparatus/places.json:9793`

**Claim.** Tradition: "Schliemann attached the tomb of Achilles — the Achilleum of the ancient sources — to this mound; the mound itself is a prehistoric settlement höyük." Note: "Schliemann took it for the tomb of Achilles."

**Evidence.** Schliemann's "Tomb of Achilles" is the conical mound on the Yenişehir / Cape Sigeion crest, excavated in 1882 after Choiseul-Gouffier's 1787 shaft (Gormezano). Burgess, "Tumuli of Achilles," *Classics@* 3: from Lechevalier 1791 onward "the largest one [on the Yenişehir slope] is labeled Achilles' tomb, while a smaller mound a few hundred meters inland is labeled Patroklos'"; "Schliemann tried his hand at it almost a century later." Cook 1973, 159–65 (quoted in secondary reports of those pages): "On the crest overlooking the sea nearly 1 km from Yenisehir is the one traditionally ascribed to Achilles." Rose and Körpe 2016 (cited in this record) treat Sivritepe, not Kumtepe, as the mound Schliemann published in *Ilios* (1881, 665–669).

Kumtepe itself is the Neolithic–EBA settlement höyük excavated by Sperling/Koşay/Blegen in 1934 and Korfmann 1993–95 (de.Wikipedia Kumtepe; Luwian Studies site 85). Schliemann did not attach Achilles to that höyük.

The gazetteer copied Livius.org "Troy 5" / Vici.org 11136 ("Heinrich Schliemann believed that this was the tomb of Achilles"), which conflate the Yenişehir tumulus with the settlement mound.

The second half of the tradition ("prehistoric settlement höyük") is true of Kumtepe and is why the Achilles attachment is the wrong mound.

**Suggested correction.** Name Schliemann's Achilles identification on the Yenişehir / Sigeion tumulus, not on Kumtepe. Keep Kumtepe as the excavated settlement höyük. Do not call it the Achilleum (that is the town; see T6).

### T2 — high — `kesik-tepe` — `apparatus/places.json:9739, 9748`

**Claim.** Tradition: "Fourth-century BC and later hero-cult identification with Achilles' tomb (Arrian, *Anabasis* 1.12)." Note: "By the 4th century BC it was regarded as Achilles' tomb, and Arrian reports Alexander sacrificing to Achilles on landing near Sigeion."

**Evidence.** Arrian 1.12 (Chinnock; Loeb 1.12.1) has Alexander at *Ilium* crowned by Menoetius and by Chares "coming from Sigeum," then "Alexander then encircled the tomb of Achilles with a garland." Arrian names no mound. The landing is 1.11, at the Port of the Achaeans, not "on landing near Sigeion" as the act of sacrifice (see T7).

Cook 1973 (epdf of pp. 165–66): "a conspicuous mound called Kesik Tepe, in which the travellers mostly recognized the Tomb of Antilochus." Schliemann in 1868 took Kesik Tepe for Festus, then in 1879 found it a natural tertiary hump.

Rose and Körpe 2016 (cited in this record), ResearchGate snippet: "Kesiktepe, linked to the tomb of Antilochus, gradually developed into a shrine for St. Demetrius." The record's own English name still carries that overlay: "the 'Demetrius tumulus'."

The 4th-century Achilles identification of *this* mound is Livius.org's inference ("This suggests that in the fourth century BCE, Kesik Tepe was regarded as the tomb of Achilles"), not Arrian and not the scholarship the record cites.

Strabo 13.1.32 puts "a temple and a monument of Achilles near Sigeium, as also monuments of Patroclus and Antilochus" — a Sigeion-area cluster, not a named identification of Kesik Tepe as Achilles.

**Suggested correction.** Either follow Cook / Rose–Körpe (Kesik Tepe = traveller "Tomb of Antilochus," later St. Demetrius) or, if an Achilles tradition is kept, name it as Livius's modern inference, not Arrian 1.12, and say the ancient testimony does not name this mound.

### T3 — high — `besik-sivritepe` — `apparatus/places.json:9768`

**Claim.** Tradition: "Hellenistic hero cult of Achilles at the Achilleion sanctuary, Beşik-Yassıtepe."

**Evidence.** Beşik-Sivritepe and Beşik-Yassıtepe are different sites, ~750 m apart. Sivritepe is the conical Hellenistic tumulus (the claimed tomb). Yassıtepe / Beşika Burnu is the Archaic settlement identified as the *town* Achilleion (Cook 1973, 185; Korfmann *AA* 1985, "Grabungen am Besik-YassiTepe und Besik-SivriTepe"; Troy Culture Route; tr.Wikipedia Beşik-Sivritepe). Vici.org itself splits them: 27329 "Achilleion [Troas] – Besik Tepe" at 39.917133, 26.159058 (the gazetteer's point) vs nearby 10832 "ACHILLEUM Besik-Yassitepe."

The record id, name, and coordinates are Sivritepe. The tradition text attaches the cult to Yassıtepe.

**Suggested correction.** Tradition on Sivritepe as the tumulus associated with Achilleion; put the town on Yassıtepe / Beşika Burnu. Do not name Yassıtepe in this record's `tradition`.

### T4 — high — `kesik-basin` — `apparatus/places.json:10012`

**Claim.** Note: "Zangger's proposed rectangular basin, roughly 330 x 230 m, functioning as a naval station or trans-shipment point."

**Evidence.** `docs/research/RESEARCH-PALEOGEOGRAPHY.md:2969–2973` and `docs/TROAD-SOURCES.md:117–120` (corrected 2026-07-29): the 330 × 230 m rectangle is the Port of Nestor at *Pylos* (Zangger and Mutlu 2015, 557–58), the analogy from which the Troy hypothesis is generalised. "No 330 × 230 m basin is claimed at Kesik." The gazetteer note reintroduces the figure as Zangger's proposal *at this cut*.

The plate layer note (`trojan-plain-schematic.json:8477`) does not repeat the dimensions.

**Suggested correction.** Drop 330 × 230 m from the Kesik note. State Zangger's Troy claim as a harbour / trans-shipment hypothesis without the Pylos measurements.

### T5 — medium — `kesik-tepe`, `kum-tepe`, `besik-sivritepe` — `places.json:9738, 9778, 9793`

**Claim.** Three records each present a "tomb of Achilles" (Kesik 4th-c. cult; Kumtepe via Schliemann; Sivritepe via Achilleion). Only `besik-sivritepe` says they conflict: "a rival 'tomb of Achilles' to Kesik Tepe." `kesik-tepe` and `kum-tepe` do not mention each other or Sivritepe.

**Evidence.** Burgess, "Tumuli of Achilles": the tomb "is a plurality"; Yenişehir (Lechevalier / Schliemann), Sivritepe (Cook / Korfmann), and other mounds have all been claimed. Strabo 13.1.32 (Sigeion) and Pliny *NH* 5.125 / Strabo 13.1.39 (Achilleion by the tomb) already encode two ancient localisations. Livius.org "Troy 5" (cited by kesik and kum) itself lists Sivritepe, Kesik, and Kum Tepe as rival candidates.

**Suggested correction.** Each Achilles-tomb record should name the others as competing traditions. Do not let the map read as three simultaneous tombs of one hero.

### T6 — medium — `kum-tepe` — `places.json:9793`

**Claim.** Tradition equates "the tomb of Achilles" with "the Achilleum of the ancient sources."

**Evidence.** Achilleion / Achilleum is the *town*, founded beside the tomb, not the tomb. Pliny *NH* 5.125: "fuit et achilleon oppidum, iuxta tumulum achillis conditum a mytilenaeis." Strabo 13.1.32: temple and monument of Achilles near Sigeium; 13.1.39 (Burgess n. 31) on the town named from the nearby tumulus. Cook and Korfmann put that town at Beşika Burnu / Yassıtepe, next to Sivritepe, not at Kumtepe.

**Suggested correction.** Do not use Achilleum as a synonym for the tomb, and do not attach the town-name to Kumtepe.

### T7 — medium — `kesik-tepe` — `places.json:9748`

**Claim.** "Arrian reports Alexander sacrificing to Achilles on landing near Sigeion."

**Evidence.** Arrian 1.11: Alexander crosses from Elaeus to the Port of the Achaeans, erects landing altars, then "went up to Ilium" and sacrificed to Athena and Priam. 1.12: still at Ilium, he is crowned; Chares arrives *from Sigeum*; then he garlands Achilles' tomb. The tomb rite is after the ascent to Ilium, not at the landing. "Near Sigeion" is a translator's footnote on the harbour (Chinnock n. 12), not Arrian's wording for the tomb.

**Suggested correction.** "Arrian 1.12: at Ilium Alexander garlanded Achilles' tomb; Chares came from Sigeum." Do not collapse landing, Sigeion, and the tomb into one act, and do not pin that act on Kesik Tepe (T2).

### T8 — medium — `besik-sivritepe` — `places.json:9768, 9778`

**Claim.** "Hellenistic hero cult of Achilles at the Achilleion sanctuary." Name: "Beşik-Sivritepe (Achilleion)."

**Evidence.** The *town* Achilleion is Archaic: Mytilenean foundation in the Sigeion war, 6th c. BC (Hdt. 5.94; Cook 1973, 185; Burgess). Korfmann dated habitation at Beşika Burnu from c. 600 BC. The *tumulus's present cone* is Hellenistic (Rose–Körpe: monumentalised second quarter of the 3rd c. BC; Vici 27329 period −330). Calling the cult "Hellenistic" and parenthesising the town-name on the tumulus collapses town, sanctuary, and mound.

**Suggested correction.** "Archaic town Achilleion at Beşika Burnu / Yassıtepe; Hellenistic monumental tumulus at Sivritepe, the mound associated with that town's Achilles cult."

### T9 — medium — `uvecik-tepe` — `places.json:9854`

**Claim.** `certainty: "certain"` for Üvecik Tepe as "the tomb of Festus, a favorite of Caracalla… built c. 214 AD."

**Evidence.** The mound is a real tumulus at the Pleiades/OSM point (39.9003, 26.1992 = Pleiades 897256486, Vici 11483). The Festus identification is Schliemann's (*Ilios* ch. XII, "Ujek Tepeh the Cenotaph of Festus"), from Herodian 4.8 and Dio Cassius 78.16.7 plus late-Roman sherds in the 1879 shaft. Cook 1973, 172–73 accepts it ("the greatest of the Trojan tumuli… identified [by Schliemann] as the tumulus raised by Caracalla for his favourite Festus"). It is the standard identification, not an inscribed certainty. House rule: `certain` is for what survey supports; an identification, even a strong one, is `traditional`.

Pleiades itself describes it as "a tumulus in the Troad built for one Festus" — i.e. it repeats the identification.

**Suggested correction.** Keep the Festus note as the named tradition; tier `traditional` (or split: mound `certain`, Festus identification `traditional`).

### T10 — medium — `kesik-basin` — `places.json:10005–10007`; plate `trojan-plain-schematic.json:8468`

**Claim.** Record `certainty: "speculative"` and `kind: "harbour"`. Tradition: "The feature — an artificial-looking cut through the 40 m Yeniköy ridge west of the plain — is certain; every reading of its purpose is speculative and contested." Plate label: "Kesik cut (contested)."

**Evidence.** The landform is on Cook's map (1973, 166–67: unfinished cutting, "the work was never completed"). Kayan 2009 treats the Kesik plain as a palaeogeographic fact with an undecided canal reading. `kind: harbour` picks Zangger's purpose, which the tradition field says is contested. `certainty: speculative` contradicts the tradition's "feature is certain." The plate label puts "contested" on the cut itself.

**Suggested correction.** Feature `certain`; purpose left to the tradition/note. `kind` something other than `harbour` (cut / canal / landform). Plate label "Kesik cut" without implying the landform's existence is in doubt.

### T11 — low — `kesik-basin` — `places.json:10012`

**Claim.** "The other readings, in hedge: a harbor candidate… an undecided, unfinished canal… and late-Roman drainage."

**Evidence.** House rule: the tier carries uncertainty; the note states facts. The note names its own hedging ("in hedge") and stacks caveats ("thin and presented as hypothesis only," "the dossier forbids," "unusable as a published coordinate"). Cook 1973, 166–67 and Kayan 2003/2009 can be stated as those authors' positions without the apology.

**Suggested correction.** One sentence per reading, with the citation; drop "in hedge" and the research-dossier asides from the reader-facing note.

### T12 — low — `tomb-of-ajax-in-tepe` — `places.json:9827` vs `9838–9848`

**Claim.** Tradition cites "Pliny, Natural History 5.125; Strabo 13.1.30." `sources` lists Pleiades, Strabo 13.1.30, and Rose 2014 — not Pliny.

**Evidence.** Pliny 5.125 is the right section (Teubner/Loeb: Aeantion, Ajax buried there, 30 stadia from Sigeum). The citation is accurate and missing from `sources`.

**Suggested correction.** Add Pliny *NH* 5.125 to `sources`.

### T13 — low — `pinarbasi` — `places.json:9981`

**Claim.** "the rival site until Schliemann."

**Evidence.** Lechevalier's 1785 identification of Kırk Göz / Bunarbashi and Ballı Dağ is right (Cook 1973, 141–46; `docs/research/RESEARCH-TROAD-TOPOGRAPHY.md:1061`). Frank Calvert was already arguing Hisarlık before Schliemann dug. "Until Schliemann" is the popular story, not the sequence of the identification.

**Suggested correction.** "the rival site until Calvert and Schliemann established Hisarlık," or "the dominant identification in the travellers until the 1860s–70s."

### T14 — low — `sigeion` — `places.json:9889, 9898`

**Claim.** `kind: "promontory"`; sole Pleiades cite is 550877 "Sigeion" (the city). `docs/TROAD-SOURCES.md:394` cited both 550877 (city) and 550878 (promontory).

**Evidence.** Historical Sigeion is a city on Cape Sigeum (Cook 1973; note correctly: settlement *and* western headland). Kind and citation pick one of the two.

**Suggested correction.** Cite 550877 and 550878, or set `kind` to settlement and keep the headland in the note.

## Confirmed

- **`sigeion`** — Name Σίγειον, not in the *Iliad*, later city on the NW headland of the Troad, traditional western horn of the beach: correct. Coords 39.9835, 26.1809 sit on the Yenişehir / Cape Sigeum ridge, NW of Hisarlık, the right side of the plain. `certain` is right for the historical city (Cook 1973). Note is plain.

- **`tomb-of-ajax-in-tepe`** — Strabo 13.1.30 Jones: "adjacent to Rhoeteium, a low-lying shore, on which are a tomb and temple of Aias." Pliny *NH* 5.125: Aeantion, Ajax buried there. Hero cult at Rhoiteion is the right tradition. Hadrianic rebuild: Philostratus *Heroicus* 8.1; Pausanias 1.35.5 (sea opened the grave; huge bones). Wikipedia Rhoiteion: vaulting in the 2nd c. AD. Coords 40.01, 26.303 are Pleiades Rhoiteion 550856, NE of the plain (correct side); the note states they are the city's point, not a survey of İn Tepe. Vici 39.9916 / 26.2420 at Kumkale is correctly rejected. `traditional` is right.

- **`besik-sivritepe` coordinates and rival-note** — 39.9171, 26.1591 matches Vici 27329 and the north end of Beşik Bay, SW of Hisarlık (correct side). Note's "rival 'tomb of Achilles' to Kesik Tepe" is the right conflict statement (incomplete only because kum-tepe is left out; T5). Hellenistic date of the *cone* matches Rose–Körpe / Korfmann.

- **`uvecik-tepe` location and Festus note** — 39.9003, 26.1992 = Pleiades 897256486 / Vici 11483, south of Troy on the Alexandria Troas road (correct side). Note's facts (Festus, Caracalla, c. 214, not a heroic burial, largest tumulus) match Schliemann *Ilios* 663, Cook 1973, 172–73, Herodian 4.8, Dio 78.16.7, Livius "Troy 5." Prose is plain. (Tier: T9.)

- **`pinarbasi`** — Lechevalier 1785, Kırk Göz at Bunarbashi / Pınarbaşı, Ballı Dağ as Troy: correct (Cook 1973, 141–46). Coords 39.888, 26.2715 are SSE of Hisarlık, the right quarter of the plain; the note flags them as a village proxy, not a spring-head. Wolkersdorfer et al. 2021 and Cook on the missing hot/cold contrast: correctly reported. `traditional` is right.

- **`thymbrios`** — Strabo 13.1.35 Jones: "the Thymbrius River, which flows through the plain and empties into the Scamander at the temple of the Thymbraean Apollo." Not named in Homer. Identification with the Kemer Su is Cook 1973, 112–13 "presumably the Kemer Su," which the note quotes. Coords 39.8997, 26.2933 lie ESE of Hisarlık at the right confluence quarter. `traditional` is right. (Pleiades 550929 vs 550927: not verified, below.)

- **`kesik-basin` as a landform** — An artificial-looking cut through the Yeniköy ridge west of the plain, with competing readings (Luce wall; Kayan unfinished canal; Cook late-Roman drainage never completed; Zangger harbour): the *set* of readings matches Cook 1973, 166–67 and the files cited. Plate lettering-only, no invented linework: matches the dossier rule. Cook's "about 500 m south of the mound" is the right direction from Kesik Tepe 39.9608, 26.1682. (Dimensions: T4; kind/tier/hedging: T10–T11.)

- **`kesik-tepe` coordinates** — 39.9608, 26.1682 = Vici 11135, west of Hisarlık on the Sigeion ridge (correct side). Note flags Vici as unchecked against a survey plan.

- **Layer group** — `trojan-plain-schematic.json:9832–9845`: id `later-tradition`, title "Later tradition and survey", `default: "off"`, the eight placeIds plus `kesik-basin`. Matches the brief.

## Not verified

- **Kumtepe excavated-höyük coordinate.** Gazetteer / OSM node 7979325496 / Vici 11136: 39.9936, 26.1926. de.Wikipedia Kumtepe and Luwian Studies: 39.9789, 26.1960, ~1.6 km south. Vici 64545 "Kumtepe" (Neolithic settlement) is a third point (39.9956, 26.2087, "location uncertain"), listed 1 km from Vici 11136. Which of these is Blegen/Korfmann's trench was not confirmed from a published survey plan. Tied to T1's conflation of "Achilles mound" and höyük.

- **`kesik-tepe` as "the most impressive mound on the Trojan plain."** Copied from Livius.org. Cook 1973 calls Üvecik "the greatest of the Trojan tumuli" (~25 m); Burgess calls Üvecik "the highest tumulus in the Troad." Heights not compared from a survey.

- **Luce 1998 reading the Kesik cut as the Achaean wall and ditch.** The note asserts it and says Kraft, Rapp, Kayan & Luce 2003 cite Kayan 1995 for it though that paper does not contain it. Luce 1998 was not opened here.

- **Kayan 1995 / 2003 / 2009 and Jablonka–Kayan water-engineering paper.** Cited; not read in this pass. Cook 1973, 166–67 on unfinished work was confirmed only from quoted pages, not the printed book.

- **Pleiades 550929** ("Thymbras Pedion") as the Thymbrios–Scamander confluence 39.8997 / 26.2933. Anubis blocked the Pleiades page. 550927 is the id in `TROAD-SOURCES.md` for Thymbra. Whether 550929 is the confluence or a representative rectangle was not confirmed.

- **Pleiades 550877** exact lon/lat vs gazetteer 39.9835, 26.1809. Direction and identity are right (Yenişehir); the published Pleiades point was not fetched.

- **Philostratus *Heroicus* as evidence for Kum Tepe or Kesik Tepe as Achilleum.** Livius.org / Vici 11136 claim this; Burgess reads Philostratus as Troad cult without pinning a modern mound. Not verified from the Greek.

- **Kesik cut "40 m Yeniköy ridge"** and Cook's "500 m south" as a measured offset. Direction is right; the numbers were not checked against Cook's printed page or a DEM.

- **Wolkersdorfer et al. 2021** 17.4–18.5 °C on 18 of 40 springs. Cited with DOI; paper not read. Cook 1973, 141–46 on travellers' thermometers is the same finding in earlier form.
