## Bibliography (PD status per US rules)

1. **Leaf**, *The Iliad*, 2nd ed., Macmillan 1900–02. **PD** — full on
   Perseus (1999.04.0056). Running notes gloss each day transition
   (1.493, 24.31, 24.107); a consolidated print-appendix table
   unconfirmed — if it exists it is reproducible outright.
2. Bassett, *The Poetry of Homer*, Sather 1938. NOT PD (to 2034).
   Cited by others for "at least fifty days."
3. Whitman, *Homer and the Heroic Tradition*, Harvard 1958. NOT PD.
   Ring symmetry: 21-day opening (1+9+12) mirrored by 21-day close
   (12+9+burial) — the structural basis of the ~51 count.
4. Kirk et al., *The Iliad: A Commentary*, CUP 1985–93. NOT PD;
   paywalled — **GAP: needs library pull for its day notes.**
5. Taplin, *Homeric Soundings*, Oxford 1992. NOT PD. Three
   performance-sections (1.1–9.13; 11.1–18.353; 18.354–24.804), each
   one day/night of performance — closest thing to a dramatized-days
   framing found.
6. de Jong, *Narrators and Focalizers*, 1987/2004. NOT PD. Organizes
   the poem around "four days of battle"; scenic vs panoramic
   narration.
7. Erickson, "How many days does Homer's Iliad cover?", Homer in the
   Laboratory blog (Brighton), 2018 — secondary synthesis table (54 by
   his count; 50–51 by others). Map only, not scholarship.

Consensus: **"at least fifty days," commonly 51 (also 50/52/54 by
inclusive/exclusive counting at the summarized spans).** No single
canonical named table located on the open web; Leaf (PD) and Kirk
(library) are the next-step primary pulls.

## The poem's own duration statements (VERIFIED verbatim)

- 1.53–54 ἐννῆμαρ … τῇ δεκάτῃ — nine days of plague, assembly on the
  tenth.
- 1.493–94 ἐκ τοῖο δυωδεκάτη γένετ᾽ ἠώς — twelfth dawn; gods return.
- 24.31 — same formula verbatim: twelve days of Hector's maltreatment.
- 24.107 ἐννῆμαρ δὴ νεῖκος ἐν ἀθανάτοισιν — a THIRD nine-day span
  (divine quarrel), running inside the twelve of 24.31.
- 24.664–667 — Priam's truce terms: 9 mourn / 10th bury+feast / 11th
  mound / 12th fight if we must.
- 24.781, 784–787 — grant + execution: nine days wood, tenth dawn
  cremation.
- **24.789–801: the mound-morning carries NO ordinal — generic
  rosy-fingered-dawn formula only — and the poem ends before the
  promised twelfth day. Homer sets a 12-day frame rhetorically and
  does not narrate it to completion. The end-compression is the
  poet's own.**

Pattern: 9/9/9 and 12/12 — stylized summary intervals, a deliberate
compositional device (supports reading frame-spans as schematic).

## The 38-vs-51 axis (for John's decision)

- Nearly all divergence lives in two frame stretches: the 12-day
  divine absence (Bk 1) and the 12+9(+2) complex ending Bk 24 —
  ~30 of the ~51 traditional days; the dramatized core is ~15–20 days.
- Indirect support for compression: Whitman's symmetry, de Jong's
  battle-day organization, Taplin's performance-day units, and the
  24.789ff. finding above.
- **No published scholar explicitly adopts a count-only-dramatized-
  days convention.** Our Day 1–38 is a novel, well-grounded editorial
  choice — precedent-SETTING, not precedent-following. The traditional
  ~51 is the citable path; ours is defensible with an explanatory
  note. John's call (queued in docs/OVERNIGHT.md).

## Quote candidates (verbatim, cited)

1. Olson (BMCR 1993.01.21, review of Taplin): "the poem is best
   understood as consisting of three long sections of narrative
   (1.1–9.13; 11.1–18.353; 18.354–24.804), each of which would have
   taken a full day (or night) to perform."
2. Same review: "not only the characters within the story...but also
   Homer's audience are being encouraged to look forward to the 'next
   day.'"
3. Il. 24.31 (=1.493): ἀλλ᾽ ὅτε δή ῥ᾽ ἐκ τοῖο δυωδεκάτη γένετ᾽ ἠώς.
4. Il. 24.107: ἐννῆμαρ δὴ νεῖκος ἐν ἀθανάτοισιν ὄρωρεν.
5. Il. 24.667: τῇ δὲ δυωδεκάτῃ πολεμίξομεν εἴ περ ἀνάγκη — the
   deadline the poem never narrates reaching.
