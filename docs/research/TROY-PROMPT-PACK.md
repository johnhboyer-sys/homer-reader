# TROY-PROMPT-PACK — Grok Imagine 2.0 prompts for the standing city

**Date:** 2026-08-10.
**Purpose:** ready-to-paste prompts for generating two artistic views of Troy VI —
the poem's standing, inhabited city — for John to run through Grok Imagine 2.0 and
curate. Not for the pipeline; not committed to any plate. A visual sketch pad, gated
entirely by the constraints below.
**Sources:** every constraint traces to `docs/research/RESEARCH-TROY-APPEARANCE.md`
(§1 poem licences, §2 elevation archaeology, §4 setting, §6 artist's brief and the
14-item DO NOT DRAW list, which is the authority used below) or to
`docs/research/RESEARCH-CITADEL.md` (footprint, gates, grid), or to Dörpfeld's own
Figur 470 (`research-cache/dorpfeld-1902-fig-470.jpg`) — his 1902 restored plan of
the citadel and its surroundings, the closest thing to an authorial composition
guide: the circuit on its spur, the Scaean gate conjectured at the northwest
corner, the Dardanian gate to the southeast, the fig-tree hill (Erineos) and the
spring just outside the wall, the roads — to the Skamander ford, to the upper
Simoeis, up to the citadel itself — and the Simoeis and Skamander valleys framing
the mound on either side. Anything below marked **[artist's licence]** is not in
either research file or the figure; it fills a gap the sources leave open and is
flagged rather than smuggled in as fact.

**Correction folded in during drafting.** An earlier draft of this brief called
the wall "not ashlar, not classical masonry." That was wrong. Troy VI's
fortification is **finely cut limestone ashlar in regular courses** — one of the
site's distinctive features, and what separates it from the rough Cyclopean
masonry of Mycenae and Tiryns. What actually distinguishes it from a *classical
Greek* wall is two things only: the face **battens** (leans inward as it rises,
13–22° from vertical — DO-NOT-DRAW item 12 in §6.2 is a vertical ashlar cliff with
no batter), and in plan it is a **polygon of long straight runs**, never a smooth
circle (DO-NOT-DRAW item 11). Per §2.1, the jogs between those runs are shallow —
segments of 7–10 m meeting at a step of only 0.10–0.15 m, occasionally 0.30 m —
so the prompts below describe the wall as *faceted*, never as *jogged*,
*offset*, or *sawtoothed*: an image model given those words draws a dramatically
serrated, bastioned circuit, which is badly wrong. The socle is 4.5–5 m thick,
with face-blocks up to 1.5 × 0.3 m — big, closely fitted, monumental. Towers and
gates stand straighter and are cut finer than the curtain around them.

## Orientation — what you're generating, and what to reject on sight

You're generating Troy VI, the Bronze Age Anatolian citadel the *Iliad* imagines
standing — around 1200 BC, on the mound of Hisarlık, a spur at the west end of a
ridge above the Trojan plain. It is a **compact fortified acropolis**, about
220 × 200 m in plan with a wall circuit of only ~550 m enclosing some two
hectares — no bigger than a couple of city blocks — ringed by a wall of **finely
cut, pale cream-buff limestone ashlar whose face leans inward as it rises**,
faceted in plan rather than a smooth curve, topped with a band of tan mudbrick.
Towers of the same pale stone, cut straighter and finer than the curtain, break
the circuit at the northeast, east, and south. Below and around it a
**lower town may spill onto the plateau** — but it is a modest, low, browner
skirt beneath the crown, ditch-cut at its outer edge, and it must **never
overpower the ashlar citadel**: the poem's own "city" is the fortifiable
citadel itself (ἄστυ / πτολίεθρον), and people simply "live in the city" — a
sprawling lower town is not something the *Iliad* describes as architecture,
so the picture should not either. The walled citadel is the subject and must
dominate the frame; the lower town, where it appears, reads as small,
subordinate, and clearly secondary. Inside the citadel, streets climb in
terraces to royal houses, shrines, and an open agora at the summit. The single
fastest tell that a returned image is wrong: **pitched, red-tile gable roofs**.
Bronze Age Troy had **flat, mud-plastered roofs** — reject any image with
terracotta tiles or ridge-lines on sight, the same way you'd reject a Roman
aqueduct or a knight in plate armor. The second fastest tell is **scale and
balance** — if the generator hands back a sprawling classical or medieval
capital with the citadel lost inside a big lower city, or a lower town that
rivals or overwhelms the walled crown, that's wrong; the citadel should read
as a small, dominant hilltop fortress you could walk around in fifteen
minutes, with at most a modest skirt of humbler housing below it. The third
tell: a **vertical, sheer-faced wall** with no lean to it — Troy's wall
battens like a rampart, it does not stand plumb like a Greek temenos wall.

## Scoring checklist

Run against every returned image. Score yes/no; an image with more than two or
three "no" answers on items 1–6 is not worth keeping regardless of how good it
looks.

1. **Roof form** — are all roofs flat and mud-plastered, with no pitched or
   red-tile roofs anywhere in the frame?
2. **Wall batter** — does the wall face visibly lean inward as it rises,
   rather than standing plumb like a classical wall?
3. **Wall plan** — does the circuit read as a polygon of long straight runs
   (faceted, catching light in flat planes), rather than a smooth perfect
   circle or a dramatically serrated zigzag?
4. **Masonry quality** — is the stone finely cut, coursed, pale cream-buff
   ashlar, not rubble, not rough fieldstone, and not glossy white marble?
5. **Scale and dominance** — does the citadel read as a compact, walkable
   hilltop fortress (roughly two city blocks) that visibly dominates the
   picture, with any lower town reading as a modest, low, subordinate skirt
   at its foot — never a sprawling metropolis, and never a lower town that
   rivals or outsizes the walled crown?
6. **Towers** — does at least one tower (ideally at the northeast) read
   taller, straighter, and more finely cut than the curtain wall around it?
7. **Street plan** (View A) — is an interior ring street and at least one
   radial street toward a gate or the summit legible?
8. **Royal house** — does the palace/royal complex read as a packed cluster
   of small stone courtyard-chambers with a colonnade, not a single classical
   megaron hall or a marble temple-palace?
9. **Shrines** — are any temple/shrine buildings small, flat-roofed, with
   plain doors and no columns or pediments?
10. **Topography** — does the city sit visibly on a spur/brow with higher
    ground behind it, the plain to the west/south, and water reading as
    marsh/lagoon/inland bay rather than a modern deep-water harbor at the
    foot of the walls?
11. **Ida** (View B) — is Mount Ida visible on the horizon as a real,
    regional, non-alpine, non-snow-capped mountain?
12. **Anachronism scan** — no fluted columns, arches, domes, crenellations,
    cobbled paving, plate armor, cavalry, stirrups, or movie-branding
    elements anywhere in the frame?
