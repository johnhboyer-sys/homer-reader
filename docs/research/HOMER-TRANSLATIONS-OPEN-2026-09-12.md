# Open-license and public-domain English translations of Homer — a 2026 survey

Prepared 2026-09-12. Question: beyond Murray, Butler, Pope, and the Kosmos
Society/Center for Hellenic Studies (CHS) revision of Butler already in use,
are there more recent English translations of the *Iliad* or *Odyssey* the
Homer Reader could lawfully use? Two routes: an explicit open license (any CC
variant, or a stated free-for-non-commercial-use permission that is not a CC
license), or US public domain by lapse of copyright, judged under 2026 US law.

Method note on evidence: the Stanford Copyright Renewal Database
(exhibits.stanford.edu/copyrightrenewals) blocks plain HTTP fetches with a bot
check; it was searched through a real browser session (Browserbase). Searches
below are quoted exactly as run. HathiTrust's catalog blocked both plain fetch
and search-result rendering during this research; where a HathiTrust record
URL was found it is given, but its rights code could not be confirmed and is
marked uncertain.

## The main finding: a URAA trap catches several of the obvious candidates

Every translation first published outside the United States before 1978 by
an author who was still alive, or dead fewer than 70 years, on 1 January 1996
had its US copyright automatically restored that day by the Uruguay Round
Agreements Act (URAA) — regardless of whether a US renewal was ever filed.
Non-renewal only frees a work if the work's country of origin is the United
States (first published there, or published in the US within 30 days of a
foreign edition, which under Berne Article 5(4) makes the US the country of
origin). This catches Rouse, Rieu, Marris's *Iliad*, and Andrew below: each
was first published in Britain by an author who outlived 1996 (or came close
enough that their home country's life-plus-70 term had not run out by then),
so the absence of a US renewal record proves nothing — the URAA restored the
US copyright anyway. The restored term for a pre-1978 published work is 95
years from first publication.

## Already-usable public-domain translations (pre-1931), for completeness

These were named in the brief as older options; they are already free to use
and require no further clearance. Listed briefly: translator, work, year,
verse or prose, and a source.

- **George Chapman** — *Iliad* (1598–1611) and *Odyssey* (1614–15), verse. Project Gutenberg and Internet Archive.
- **Alexander Pope** — already in use by the site.
- **William Cowper** — *Iliad* and *Odyssey* (1791), blank verse. Wikisource and Project Gutenberg.
- **Edward Smith-Stanley, 14th Earl of Derby** — *Iliad* (1864), blank verse. Project Gutenberg #6150.
- **William Cullen Bryant** — *Iliad* and *Odyssey* (1870s), blank verse. Standard Ebooks and Project Gutenberg.
- **Theodore Alois Buckley** — *Iliad* (1851), prose. Wikisource ("The Iliad of Homer (Buckley)") and Project Gutenberg.
- **S. H. Butcher & Andrew Lang** — *Odyssey* (1879), prose. Project Gutenberg #1728.
- **Andrew Lang, Walter Leaf & Ernest Myers** — *Iliad* (1883), prose. Project Gutenberg #6130.
- **George Herbert Palmer** — *Odyssey* (1884), prose.
- **Arthur S. Way** — *Odyssey* (1880) and *Iliad*, verse. Internet Archive; also mirrored at johnstoniatexts.x10host.com/homer/wayodyssey.htm.
- **H. B. Cotterill** — *Odyssey* (1911), prose.
- **Samuel Butler's own editions** — already the basis of the site's current Butler text; no separate clearance needed.

General caveat for this group: most 19th-century verse translations compress
or expand lines relative to the Greek and were not printed with per-line
Greek numbering; the prose translations (Buckley, Butcher & Lang, Palmer,
Butler) are typically divided by book and paragraph rather than by Greek
line number. Confirm line-alignment format against the specific edition
before building apparatus around any of these.

## Ranked recommendation

1. **Ian Johnston's *Iliad* and *Odyssey*.** Explicit, author-stated public-domain dedication, both epics complete, modern and readable prose-leaning verse, hosted in clean HTML. The one open question is a secondary note (found in a search snippet, not on the primary license page itself) reserving commercial-publishing rights — worth a short email to Johnston confirming a non-commercial reader site is covered, but this is the strongest candidate found.

2. **A. S. Kline's *Iliad* and *Odyssey*.** A clear, quotable non-commercial-use permission (not CC, so label it correctly on the site), both epics complete, verse, actively maintained site. Same caveat as Johnston: since this is a personal permission grant rather than an irrevocable license, a confirming email is worth sending before committing engineering time.

3. **William Marris's 1925 *Odyssey*.** Genuinely public domain outright (pre-1931, no analysis required), verse, and gives a second verse option distinct from Pope. Its companion 1934 *Iliad* is not yet free (URAA-restored to 2029) — do not pair them without separately clearing the *Iliad*.

4. **T. E. Lawrence's 1932 *Odyssey* — flagged for a follow-up records check, not yet a green light.** If the London and New York 1932 editions turn out to have been simultaneous (within 30 days), Berne's rule makes this a US work, and the lack of any US renewal (confirmed by three separate Stanford database searches) would put it in the public domain now. That would be a genuine find: a widely admired prose *Odyssey* by a well-known translator, free to use immediately. If they were not simultaneous, it is URAA-restored and unusable until 2028. This is worth a targeted next step — a Library of Congress or WorldCat record with exact months, or a specialist rare-book description — before either using or discarding it.

Recommendation 5 is deliberately left open: nothing else surveyed (Rouse, Rieu, Chase & Perry, Rees, Andrew, the Marris *Iliad*) clears either route, and no OER or Wikisource source offers a new translation beyond what the site already has.

## What could not be settled

- **T. E. Lawrence's *Odyssey*** (see above) — the exact 1932 publication dates in London and New York were not found in the sources searched; this determines whether it is free to use now or restored until 2028.
- **W. H. D. Rouse** — the analysis here rests on the Modern Age Books (New York) edition being a later, licensed US reprint rather than a simultaneous release; this was not confirmed with a precise date, only inferred from the different publisher name and the general pattern of UK-first publication for Rouse's Nelson editions.
- **HathiTrust rights codes** — catalog.hathitrust.org returned HTTP 403 to both direct fetch and search-page rendering during this research (for Rouse, Rieu, and the T. E. Lawrence records); no rights code (pd / pdus / ic) could be independently confirmed there. A manual check by someone with normal browser access to HathiTrust would strengthen or correct the Rouse and Lawrence entries above.
- **S. O. Andrew's Everyman's Library volume** — the UK-first-publication assumption for this specific joint Andrew/Oakley edition was not independently verified; Everyman's Library published from both London and New York at various points, and if a US-first or simultaneous edition existed the analysis would change.
- **Ian Johnston's commercial-publishing caveat** — found only in a secondary description, not confirmed against his primary license page; the practical effect on a donation-supported but non-commercial site is unclear without asking him directly.
